# Flight direction — supersedes static/gliding storyboard

User correction: this must feel like a first-person drone journey through distinct worlds, not a Ken Burns slideshow. Camera movement is the story. Each world has a different cast, architecture, palette and scale.

## Route and timing

0–2 seconds: Lift from the notebook beside the orange cat. Accelerate toward the open attic window. Curtain passes close enough to occlude the lens briefly.

2–5 seconds: Continue the same forward/right movement outside a Tokyo apartment. Pass the fox and red panda at their low table. Bank into the railway corridor while an approaching train passes in the opposite direction. Keep the camera moving forward; do not reverse to follow this train. Its near side or a close structural column supplies the transition wipe.

5–8 seconds: Emerge from behind a tree trunk in a bright forest, retaining speed and screen direction. Move from a slightly overhead view into a level swoop above the stream. Pass bunny/turtle/owl on the branch, then use a NEAR trunk as the boundary cut. The distant arch is scenery, not a destination to reach within three seconds; racing the whole valley would ruin scale and pacing.

8–12 seconds: Exit into the open neon city canyon. Start with a wider controlled descent between towers; arc along the EXTERIOR of the glass study pod with capybara, honey bear and paper ghost. Slow for their silhouettes to register, then accelerate toward the actual warm doorway at center-left. Never travel through the closed pod glass. An opaque doorway edge hides the next cut.

12–15 seconds: Cross the doorway into Rainy Library, lower to desk height and decelerate smoothly to rest. Only the sleeping orange cat returns. Clean composited nook wordmark and exact tagline. Hold the end clearly long enough to read. If fifteen seconds cannot support legible characters and a calm end, trim travel beats first rather than increasing every shot's speed.

## Production approach

Generate short source shots with deliberate beginning and ending camera states. These stills establish each environment and character placement; they are not a complete set of transition endpoint frames yet. Generate endpoint plates as required by the actual Higgsfield model schema after motion method is selected. Prefer occlusion cuts with matched screen direction and speed. Do not instruct the model to dissolve creatures or morph buildings into trees.

Use near/mid/far parallax, eased speed ramps, curved paths, small banks and controlled vertical movement. No stationary frames disguised by zooming, no random spins, no clipping through solid glass or furniture. Characters stay occupied with studying; scenery provides travel energy.

V2 cast: orange cat at opening/closing; fox + red panda in Tokyo; scarf bunny + moss turtle + owl in forest; capybara + honey bear + paper ghost in neon study pod. Only one of each per scene. Creature animations remain subtle while the camera does the travelling.

Validation must inspect the actual animated frames: stable anatomy, no humans introduced, geometry continuity, no camera/object collisions, readable subjects, matched boundary velocity, level recovery after banks, no flashes, clean deceleration, and readable end card. A successful still does not verify motion.
